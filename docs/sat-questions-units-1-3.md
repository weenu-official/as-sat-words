# Digital SAT 형식 문제 검수용 목록 (1~3유닛, 60문제)

각 문제의 **A가 정답**입니다 (앱에서는 보기 순서가 매번 섞입니다). 검수할 때 확인할 것:

1. 정답이 지문에 논리적으로 딱 맞는가
2. 오답 3개 중 정답으로도 읽힐 수 있는 것이 없는가
3. 문장이 자연스러운가 (원어민 기준)
4. 해설(Why)이 지문의 단서를 정확히 짚는가

수정이 필요하면 단어와 문제 번호(예: tedious #2)로 알려주세요.

## Unit 1

### 1. unimportant

**#1** For decades, biologists treated the long stretches of DNA that do not code for proteins as ______, calling them "junk." Recent studies, however, show that many of these regions help control when genes switch on and off.

- A) unimportant ✅
- B) indispensable
- C) recognizable
- D) vigorous

> Why: Calling the DNA "junk" shows that scientists thought it had little significance; "however" signals that this view later changed.

**#2** In her notebook, the novelist recorded details that might seem ______ to anyone else: the color of a neighbor's gate, the price of bread. Years later, those small observations gave her fiction its convincing sense of place.

- A) unimportant ✅
- B) conclusive
- C) lucrative
- D) infallible

> Why: The examples are small, everyday details that most people would consider insignificant, yet they proved valuable later.

### 2. comparable

**#1** The two telescopes were built decades apart and use entirely different mirrors. Even so, tests show that the images they produce are ______ in sharpness, differing by less than one percent.

- A) comparable ✅
- B) atypical
- C) inaccessible
- D) arbitrary

> Why: "Even so" and "differing by less than one percent" show that the images are roughly equal in quality.

**#2** Economists caution against assuming that wages in the two cities are ______. Although the average salaries look similar, housing in one city costs nearly twice as much, so the same paycheck buys far less.

- A) comparable ✅
- B) tedious
- C) implicit
- D) spontaneous

> Why: The salaries only "look similar"; the warning is against treating them as truly equivalent.

### 3. recognizable

**#1** After two centuries underwater, the ship's wooden hull had almost entirely rotted away. Only the iron anchor remained ______, its familiar shape still clear beneath a thin layer of coral.

- A) recognizable ✅
- B) lucrative
- C) communicative
- D) superfluous

> Why: A "familiar shape" that is "still clear" is one that can easily be identified.

**#2** The composer's style is so ______ that listeners can often identify her work within a few seconds: sudden silences, followed by a single low note on the cello.

- A) recognizable ✅
- B) obscure
- C) tedious
- D) precarious

> Why: Listeners can "identify her work within a few seconds," so the style is easy to recognize.

### 4. infallible

**#1** Early users trusted the navigation software as if it were ______. That confidence faded after the program directed several drivers onto a road that had been closed for years.

- A) infallible ✅
- B) atypical
- C) unintelligible
- D) consequential

> Why: Users trusted the software completely, as though it could not make mistakes, until it made an obvious one.

**#2** The historian reminds readers that eyewitness accounts are not ______: memories fade, and people often describe what they expected to see rather than what actually happened.

- A) infallible ✅
- B) unimportant
- C) comparable
- D) elaborate

> Why: Fading memories and mistaken descriptions are reasons that eyewitnesses can be wrong, so their accounts are not free of error.

### 5. inspect

**#1** Before any bridge in the state reopens after an earthquake, engineers must ______ every support cable by hand, looking for cracks too small to appear in photographs.

- A) inspect ✅
- B) depict
- C) circulate
- D) reciprocate

> Why: "Looking for cracks" by hand describes examining something closely to find problems.

**#2** Customs officers at the port do not have time to ______ each of the thousands of containers that arrive daily. Instead, they examine a small sample chosen by a risk-scoring program.

- A) inspect ✅
- B) conceive
- C) elevate
- D) cultivate

> Why: "Instead, they examine a small sample" restates the blank: officers cannot closely check every container.

### 6. elevate

**#1** Because the river floods nearly every spring, builders in the delta ______ new houses on concrete posts, keeping the living areas two meters above the ground.

- A) elevate ✅
- B) accumulate
- C) disengage
- D) speculate

> Why: Houses kept "two meters above the ground" have been raised to a higher position.

**#2** Critics once dismissed quilting as a mere household craft. A 2002 museum exhibition helped ______ it to the status of fine art, placing quilts beside paintings in the main gallery.

- A) elevate ✅
- B) confine
- C) inspect
- D) circulate

> Why: Moving from "a mere household craft" to "fine art" is a rise in status.

### 7. reciprocate

**#1** Cleaner fish remove parasites from larger fish, which ______ by not eating them. Biologists cite the arrangement as a classic case in which both species benefit.

- A) reciprocate ✅
- B) speculate
- C) accumulate
- D) disengage

> Why: Both species benefit: the larger fish return the favor by leaving the cleaner fish unharmed.

**#2** When one country lowered its tariffs on imported steel, it expected its trading partner to ______. Instead, the partner kept its own tariffs in place, and the talks stalled.

- A) reciprocate ✅
- B) intrigue
- C) depict
- D) surpass

> Why: The country expected a similar action in return; "Instead" shows that the partner did not respond in kind.

### 8. tedious

**#1** Archivists at the museum spent months copying thousands of handwritten shipping records. Although the work was ______, requiring the same careful steps for every page, it revealed trade routes that historians had long overlooked.

- A) tedious ✅
- B) lucrative
- C) atypical
- D) infallible

> Why: Months of repeating "the same careful steps for every page" describes work that is long, slow, and repetitive.

**#2** Counting pollen grains under a microscope is ______ work: a single sample can take a researcher six hours of nearly identical motions. A new imaging program now completes the same count in minutes.

- A) tedious ✅
- B) conclusive
- C) vigorous
- D) recognizable

> Why: "Six hours of nearly identical motions" points to work that is tiring because it is so repetitive.

### 9. lucrative

**#1** When the canal opened in 1825, merchants who had struggled to move grain overland suddenly found the trade ______: shipping costs fell by nearly ninety percent, and profits rose accordingly.

- A) lucrative ✅
- B) tedious
- C) comparable
- D) unimportant

> Why: Falling costs and rising profits show that the trade began producing a great deal of money.

**#2** Few poets expect their work to be ______. Most support themselves by teaching or editing, since even a prizewinning collection rarely sells more than a few thousand copies.

- A) lucrative ✅
- B) unintelligible
- C) inaccessible
- D) communicative

> Why: Poets need other jobs because poetry sells poorly; in other words, it is not profitable.

### 10. atypical

**#1** Most owls hunt at night, but the northern hawk owl is ______: it is active during the day and relies on sight more than hearing to find its prey.

- A) atypical ✅
- B) infallible
- C) tedious
- D) comparable

> Why: "Most owls... but" sets up a contrast: this owl differs from what is normal for owls.

**#2** The doctor noted that the patient's symptoms were ______ for the illness. People with this infection usually develop a fever within two days, but she had none after a full week.

- A) atypical ✅
- B) conclusive
- C) recognizable
- D) consequential

> Why: The patient did not show what people "usually" show, so her symptoms were not the expected ones.

## Unit 2

### 11. generalization

**#1** The claim that "students prefer online classes" is a ______ drawn from a survey of only forty people at a single university. A broader study would be needed before anyone could apply it to students everywhere.

- A) generalization ✅
- B) distraction
- C) contradiction
- D) projection

> Why: A broad statement about all students based on only forty people is a conclusion drawn from limited examples.

**#2** The biologist warns against the ______ that all deep-sea animals grow slowly. Her team has found several species of squid near the ocean floor that reach full size within a year.

- A) generalization ✅
- B) justification
- C) occurrence
- D) devotion

> Why: "All deep-sea animals grow slowly" is a sweeping statement, and the fast-growing squid show that it does not always hold.

### 12. intrigue

**#1** The carved stone ______ archaeologists because its symbols match no known writing system. Dozens of researchers have since traveled to the site hoping to work out what it says.

- A) intrigued ✅
- B) reassured
- C) depicted
- D) surpassed

> Why: Researchers traveling to the site to work out the symbols shows that the stone made them very curious.

**#2** The novel is set in a royal court full of ______: ministers trade secrets, form hidden alliances, and quietly work to remove their rivals from power.

- A) intrigue ✅
- B) indifference
- C) comprehension
- D) abstraction

> Why: Secrets, hidden alliances, and quiet plots to gain power describe secret scheming.

### 13. surpass

**#1** Engineers expected the new battery to last about ten hours. In testing, it ______ that estimate, powering the device for nearly fourteen hours on a single charge.

- A) surpassed ✅
- B) asserted
- C) inspected
- D) cultivated

> Why: Fourteen hours is more than the expected ten, so the battery exceeded the estimate.

**#2** For years, analysts doubted that solar power could ever ______ coal as the region's main source of electricity. Last year it did, supplying 41 percent of the total compared with coal's 35.

- A) surpass ✅
- B) reciprocate
- C) depict
- D) conceive

> Why: Solar supplied 41 percent to coal's 35, meaning it became greater than coal.

### 14. distraction

**#1** Researchers found that students who kept phones on their desks, even face down, scored lower on memory tests. The mere presence of the device apparently served as a ______, pulling attention from the task.

- A) distraction ✅
- B) generalization
- C) justification
- D) proposition

> Why: Something "pulling attention from the task" is exactly what a distraction is.

**#2** The architect designed the library's reading room without windows at eye level, reasoning that a view of the busy street would be a ______ for people trying to concentrate.

- A) distraction ✅
- B) contradiction
- C) similarity
- D) competence

> Why: A busy street would draw attention away from reading, which is a problem for "people trying to concentrate."

### 15. circulate

**#1** In a greenhouse, fans ______ warm air from the ceiling down to the plants, preventing cold pockets from forming near the floor on winter nights.

- A) circulate ✅
- B) accumulate
- C) speculate
- D) disengage

> Why: Fans keep air moving continuously through the space, from the ceiling down to the floor.

**#2** Long before the newspaper printed the story, rumors of the factory's closing had begun to ______ among workers, passed along in break rooms and parking lots.

- A) circulate ✅
- B) surpass
- C) elevate
- D) conceive

> Why: Rumors "passed along" from person to person are spreading through the group.

### 16. disengage

**#1** Teachers in the study noticed that when lessons moved too quickly, struggling students tended to ______: they stopped asking questions and no longer joined class discussions.

- A) disengage ✅
- B) reciprocate
- C) elaborate
- D) accumulate

> Why: Students who stop asking questions and stop joining discussions have withdrawn from involvement.

**#2** Before repairs can begin, the mechanic must ______ the engine from the transmission, releasing six bolts so that the two parts can be lifted out separately.

- A) disengage ✅
- B) cultivate
- C) depict
- D) assert

> Why: "Releasing six bolts" so the parts come out separately means detaching one from the other.

### 17. cultivate

**#1** Farmers in the valley have learned to ______ rice on steep hillsides by cutting the slopes into flat steps that hold water after each rain.

- A) cultivate ✅
- B) inspect
- C) circulate
- D) speculate

> Why: Preparing the hillsides so that rice can be grown there is cultivating a crop.

**#2** The orchestra's director worked for years to ______ a relationship with local schools, visiting classrooms each month and inviting students to rehearsals. Attendance by young people has since tripled.

- A) cultivate ✅
- B) disengage
- C) surpass
- D) conceive

> Why: Years of visits and invitations show a relationship being developed through steady effort.

### 18. unintelligible

**#1** The radio message from the expedition was so broken by static that it was ______. Rescuers could make out only a few scattered words and had no idea where the team was.

- A) unintelligible ✅
- B) conclusive
- C) lucrative
- D) vigorous

> Why: Rescuers could catch only scattered words and learned nothing, so the message could not be understood.

**#2** To a visitor, the notes that chess players write during a match look ______: rows of letters and numbers with no obvious meaning. To the players, each line records a move exactly.

- A) unintelligible ✅
- B) infallible
- C) consequential
- D) communicative

> Why: "No obvious meaning" tells us the notes are impossible for a visitor to understand.

### 19. conclusive

**#1** The fossil suggested that the animal could fly, but the evidence was not ______. Only when a second specimen with preserved wing feathers turned up did scientists consider the question settled.

- A) conclusive ✅
- B) tedious
- C) atypical
- D) elaborate

> Why: The question was "settled" only later, so the first evidence did not prove the point beyond doubt.

**#2** A single experiment rarely provides ______ proof of a theory. Researchers generally wait until several independent teams have produced the same result before treating a claim as established.

- A) conclusive ✅
- B) recognizable
- C) lucrative
- D) inaccessible

> Why: Researchers wait for repeated results before calling a claim "established," because one experiment is not decisive.

### 20. conceive

**#1** The engineer first ______ the idea for the folding bridge while watching a child open and close a paper fan. It took another six years to turn that image into a working design.

- A) conceived ✅
- B) inspected
- C) circulated
- D) surpassed

> Why: The moment an idea first forms in someone's mind is when it is conceived.

**#2** Before telescopes, few people could ______ of a universe containing billions of galaxies. The night sky seemed to hold a few thousand stars and little else.

- A) conceive ✅
- B) disengage
- C) reciprocate
- D) accumulate

> Why: People could not imagine a universe so large; "conceive of" means to form such an idea in the mind.

## Unit 3

### 21. elaborate

**#1** The male bowerbird builds an ______ structure to attract a mate, arranging hundreds of twigs into an arch and decorating the entrance with shells, berries, and bits of colored glass.

- A) elaborate ✅
- B) atypical
- C) unimportant
- D) inaccessible

> Why: Hundreds of twigs plus careful decoration describe something detailed, complex, and carefully planned.

**#2** In the interview, the senator said only that the plan had "problems." When reporters asked her to ______, she declined, promising a full explanation the following week.

- A) elaborate ✅
- B) reciprocate
- C) circulate
- D) accumulate

> Why: Reporters wanted more detail than the single word "problems"; she promised that fuller explanation later.

### 22. inaccessible

**#1** Heavy snow leaves the mountain village ______ for four months each year. During that time, supplies arrive only by helicopter, and residents cannot drive to the nearest town.

- A) inaccessible ✅
- B) recognizable
- C) lucrative
- D) communicative

> Why: If supplies come only by helicopter and no one can drive out, the village is very difficult to reach.

**#2** The physicist's early papers were ______ to most readers, filled with equations and unexplained terms. Her later book, written for a general audience, finally made the same ideas clear.

- A) inaccessible ✅
- B) conclusive
- C) comparable
- D) infallible

> Why: The later book "finally made the same ideas clear," so the early papers were hard for readers to understand.

### 23. vigorous

**#1** The proposal to close the library met ______ opposition: hundreds of residents filled the council meeting, and more than five thousand signed a petition within a week.

- A) vigorous ✅
- B) tedious
- C) unintelligible
- D) atypical

> Why: A packed meeting and five thousand signatures in a week show opposition that was strong and energetic.

**#2** Tomato plants given the new fertilizer showed ______ growth, adding nearly twice as many leaves in a month as plants grown in ordinary soil.

- A) vigorous ✅
- B) inaccessible
- C) communicative
- D) conclusive

> Why: Twice as many leaves in a month is strong, energetic growth.

### 24. communicative

**#1** Honeybees are remarkably ______ insects. By performing a "dance" inside the hive, a returning bee can tell others the direction and distance of a patch of flowers.

- A) communicative ✅
- B) lucrative
- C) infallible
- D) unimportant

> Why: A bee that can "tell others" where flowers are is sharing information.

**#2** Patients rated the clinic highly because its doctors were ______: they explained each test in plain language and answered questions without hurrying.

- A) communicative ✅
- B) atypical
- C) vigorous
- D) elaborate

> Why: Explaining clearly and answering questions shows doctors who share information openly.

### 25. depict

**#1** The cave paintings ______ herds of horses and bison in motion, their legs drawn in several positions at once as if the artist wanted to show them running.

- A) depict ✅
- B) inspect
- C) surpass
- D) cultivate

> Why: Paintings that "show" animals running are representing them in a picture.

**#2** Historians note that the film does not accurately ______ life in the mining town. Real miners worked twelve-hour shifts, yet the characters seem to have endless free time.

- A) depict ✅
- B) accumulate
- C) reciprocate
- D) disengage

> Why: The complaint is about how the film shows, or represents, life in the town.

### 26. speculate

**#1** Because no written records survive, historians can only ______ about why the city was abandoned. Drought, disease, and war have each been proposed, but none has been proven.

- A) speculate ✅
- B) elaborate
- C) circulate
- D) reciprocate

> Why: With no records and nothing proven, historians can only form theories without firm evidence.

**#2** During the 1840s, thousands of ordinary investors rushed to ______ in railway companies, many of which had not yet laid a single track. When prices collapsed, most lost their savings.

- A) speculate ✅
- B) cultivate
- C) depict
- D) inspect

> Why: Putting money into unproven companies in the hope of a large profit is speculating.

### 27. accumulate

**#1** Dust and pollen ______ slowly on the lake bottom, forming a new layer of mud each year. By counting the layers, scientists can date events from thousands of years ago.

- A) accumulate ✅
- B) speculate
- C) disengage
- D) reciprocate

> Why: Material that builds up layer by layer over the years is gradually gathering.

**#2** Over forty years, the librarian ______ more than nine thousand letters written by local families, a collection now considered the best record of daily life in the region.

- A) accumulated ✅
- B) depicted
- C) surpassed
- D) conceived

> Why: A collection that grew to nine thousand letters over forty years was gathered gradually over time.

### 28. consequential

**#1** Of all the laws passed that year, the education act proved the most ______: within a decade it had doubled the number of children attending school.

- A) consequential ✅
- B) tedious
- C) unintelligible
- D) recognizable

> Why: Doubling school attendance is a significant result, so the law was important for its effects.

**#2** What looked like a minor design choice turned out to be highly ______. Moving the button two centimeters to the left cut user errors by half.

- A) consequential ✅
- B) infallible
- C) inaccessible
- D) lucrative

> Why: A choice that seemed "minor" had a large effect, cutting errors by half.

### 29. assert

**#1** In her 1962 book, the biologist ______ that pesticides were harming far more than insects. Chemical companies disputed the claim, but later studies supported her.

- A) asserted ✅
- B) inspected
- C) cultivated
- D) surpassed

> Why: She made a firm "claim" that others disputed, which means she stated it confidently as true.

**#2** After decades under the control of a distant capital, the province began to ______ its independence, creating its own courts and collecting its own taxes.

- A) assert ✅
- B) depict
- C) accumulate
- D) conceive

> Why: Setting up its own courts and taxes is acting forcefully to establish its independence.

### 30. abstraction

**#1** To young children, "justice" is an ______ that is hard to grasp. They understand it more easily through a concrete example, such as sharing toys equally.

- A) abstraction ✅
- B) occurrence
- C) objection
- D) estimation

> Why: "Justice" is a general idea, contrasted here with "a concrete example."

**#2** The mathematician was known for his ______: absorbed in a problem, he once walked past his own house three times without noticing.

- A) abstraction ✅
- B) generalization
- C) competence
- D) resentment

> Why: Being so absorbed that he missed his own house shows a state of being lost in thought.
